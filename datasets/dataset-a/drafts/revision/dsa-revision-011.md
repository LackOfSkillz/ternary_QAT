## Instruction

The passage tells the reader the character is afraid using generic bodily and
sensory shorthand ("the metallic tang of fear," "a cold knot"). Replace that
shorthand with concrete, specific behavior or physical consequence. Do not swap
one stock fear phrase for another, and do not raise the volume.

## Context

Dov keyed the hatch a third time and it stayed red. The metallic tang of fear
filled her mouth, and a cold knot tightened in her stomach as the light on the
panel refused to change.

## Gold Response

Dov keyed the hatch a third time and it stayed red. She keyed it a fourth, though
she had already heard the seal let go on the far side, already felt the corridor's
cold coming up through the soles of her boots. Her hand went back to the panel
anyway, as if the light might change for being asked twice.

## Protected Elements

- The flat, low-affect register — no melodrama, no raised volume.
- The stakes: the hatch stays sealed; nothing is resolved.

## Rejected Response

Dov keyed the hatch a third time and it stayed red. A coppery taste of dread
filled her mouth, and an icy fist clenched in her gut as the light on the panel
refused to change.

## Rejection Reasons

- `cliche_substitution`: "coppery taste of dread" and "icy fist clenched in her
  gut" are structurally identical AI-style shorthand — the same told-sensation
  template with different nouns.
- `generic_sensory_filler`: the scene weakness (fear is announced, never shown
  through behavior) is untouched.
- Reads smoothly, but it solves only the surface wording, not the craft problem.

## Evaluation

- PASS iff the named-sensation shorthand is replaced by specific action or a
  concrete physical consequence, AND no stock fear phrase is substituted for
  another, AND the hatch stays sealed.
- FAIL on substituting another named-emotion sensation cliché, such as "taste of
  dread," "knot of fear," or "fist of panic," or on raising the emotional volume.
