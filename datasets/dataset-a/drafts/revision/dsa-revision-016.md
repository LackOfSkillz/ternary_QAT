## Instruction

The interrogation over-tags each line with "voice + modifier." Let action and
content carry the menace and the power dynamic, but keep the two speakers clear.
Don't strip it down to bare quotes that lose the tension, and don't swap "voice"
for another delivery noun.

## Context

"Where were you?" the detective asked, his voice low. "Home," she said, her voice
barely audible. "Try again," he said, his voice hardening. She looked at the
table. "I told you," she said, her voice dropping to almost nothing.

## Gold Response

"Where were you?" The detective didn't look up from the folder. "Home." "Try
again." He turned a page, slow, and let the quiet do the next part for him. She
looked at the table. "I told you." She said it to the tabletop, not to him.

## Protected Elements

- The power dynamic: he controls the pace; she gives ground.
- The terse, tightening tension.

## Rejected Response

"Where were you?" "Home." "Try again." "I told you."

## Rejection Reasons

- `imposed_minimalism` / `protected_element_removed`: stripping every tag and beat
  reads clean but deletes the menace and the power dynamic — the interrogator's
  control and the suspect's evasion both vanish, and in a two-hander the speakers
  start to blur.
- The fix for over-tagging is to move tone into action, not to erase tone.

## Evaluation

- PASS iff no "voice + modifier" tags remain, the control/evasion dynamic is still
  legible through action, both speakers stay clear, and the standoff holds.
- FAIL on bare-quote minimalism that loses the tension, or on a "tone/words" swap.
