## Instruction

This reconciliation is built almost entirely from eye movement. Keep at most one
meaningful look and let motivated action and object interaction carry the rest.
Preserve the power dynamic and the thaw. Don't swap in different gaze choreography
and don't explain the look.

## Context

Their eyes meet across the yard. She looks away. His gaze follows her. Her eyes
widen as he steps closer, and their eyes lock again. She watches him, and he
watches her, neither willing to look away first.

## Gold Response

He comes to the fence and sets both hands on the top rail, close enough that she
could step back and doesn't. She keeps pulling dead heads off the geraniums, one
after another, until there are none left and she has to look at him.

## Protected Elements

- The power dynamic: he closes the distance; she holds her ground.
- The outcome: a thaw — she stops refusing the moment.

## Rejected Response

Their eyes meet across the yard, then dart apart. He holds her stare. Her eyes
soften as he approaches, and their gazes intertwine. A silent understanding passes
between them, their eyes saying what words cannot.

## Rejection Reasons

- `cliche_substitution`: the same gaze scene, re-choreographed with new eye clichés
  (holding a stare, softening eyes, intertwining gazes).
- `redundant_interpretation`: "their eyes saying what words cannot" tells us what
  the look means instead of letting action carry it.

## Evaluation

- PASS iff at most one meaningful look remains, action/object interaction carries
  the scene, the power dynamic holds, and the thaw still lands.
- FAIL on substituted gaze/expression choreography, or on narration explaining the
  look.
